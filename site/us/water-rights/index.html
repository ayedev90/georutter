United States water rights data sources

Where can I get the register of water abstraction rights and concessions in the United States, with location, holder, use, authorised volume and status?

Answer

There is no national register of water rights in the United States; each state creates and publishes its own. California (CalWATRS files on data.ca.gov, weekly CSV and API), Colorado (CDSS REST API, anonymous quota) and Oregon (OWRD point-of-diversion layers plus the WRIS record viewer) were tested. They give location, use, amounts and status, and each has a holder field. Vocabularies, units and row definitions differ by state. The multi-state WaDE/WestDAAT service was taken offline in October 2026.

Local search terms

  • water right — Legal entitlement to divert or use water, created and recorded by each state.
  • point of diversion (POD) — Location where water is taken from a source.
  • face value — California's approved annual amount of a right, in acre-feet.
  • prior appropriation — Doctrine ranking rights by priority date: first in time, first in right.
  • riparian right — California right tied to land bordering a watercourse; often has no face value.
  • WDID — Colorado DWR unique structure identifier.
  • decreed use / net amount — Colorado court-decreed use code and summed decreed amount, absolute or conditional.
  • WRIS — Oregon Water Rights Information System.

Main caveats

  • No national register exists in the evidence found: rights are recorded by each state, and only California, Colorado and Oregon were tested.
  • California's older eWRIMS flat files are marked discontinued (not updated since July 2025); the current CalWATRS datasets cover participants and geospatial links.
  • Row counts are not right counts: CA rows are right-feature links, CO rows are decreed lines per structure, OR rows are point-of-diversion by use rows.
  • Volumes are not comparable: CA face value in acre-feet, CO CFS or AF by decree, OR mostly cfs. None measures actual extraction.
  • WSWC took the WaDE/WestDAAT multi-state service offline in October 2026 and directs users to state agencies, which remain authoritative.
  • Holder fields hold personal data; names and addresses were inspected only as counts and are not reproduced.
