Federal law is consolidated officially: eCFR (regulations, current to 2026-11-24, versions since 2017, API) and the U.S. Code (statutes through Public Law 119-142, XML). They hold e.g. FERC hydro licensing and exemptions for eligible projects up to 10 MW, QF 80 MW limit, offshore renewables, BLM rights-of-way and NEPA deadlines; CEQ's NEPA rules were removed in 2025. State authorization is in each state's code (e.g. MN 216I, NY PSL Art. 8 at 25 MW); local zoning is only compiled non-officially (NLR wind ordinances). Interconnection rules sit in FERC tariffs.

Confidence reason: Federal sources tested via API and downloads; state law sampled in 4 of 50 states (California blocked) and local rules only through a non-official compilation.

Local search terms

  • Electronic Code of Federal Regulations (eCFR) — Continuously updated consolidated federal regulations; query by title and part.
  • Release point — Dated update of the United States Code downloads, named by the last Public Law included.
  • Certificate of public convenience and necessity (CCN) — State commission authorization for utility facilities such as transmission lines (e.g. Texas).
  • Siting permit / site permit — State authorization for large generation or transmission facilities (e.g. NY PSL Art. 8, MN 216I).
  • NEPA, EIS, EA — National Environmental Policy Act; environmental impact statement; environmental assessment.
  • FAST-41 — Title 41 of the FAST Act, federal coordination for covered infrastructure projects (42 U.S.C. 4370m).
  • LWECS — Large wind energy conversion system, Minnesota category of 5,000 kW or more.
  • Qualifying facility (QF) — PURPA small power production facility up to 80 MW under 18 CFR 292.204.

Main caveats

  • Permitting is split by level: federal review applies only with a federal nexus (federal land, funding, licence); states and localities authorize most onshore wind and solar.
  • CEQ NEPA regulations (40 CFR 1500-1508) were removed effective 2025-04-11; older guidance citing them is outdated.
  • Generator interconnection procedures are in each transmission provider's FERC tariff, not in the CFR.
  • Local zoning rules have no official national consolidation; the NLR compilation is AI-assisted and non-official.
