Confidence
Medium

No national official dataset of fiber routes was found; FCC and carrier sources publish availability or generalized maps, not cable routes. Official route geometry exists only for subsets: NTIA's layer of 35 federally funded Middle Mile projects (recipient and project-level progress), California's state middle-mile network with segment status (Pre-Construction, Installation, Ready-to-Connect), and some state DOT or city ITS fiber layers such as Colorado DOT's 3,832 cables with owner and lessees. Commercial long-haul routes are not openly downloadable.

Confidence reason: Official subsets were tested; the national negative rests on NTIA, HIFLD archive, carrier pages and searches, but FCC pages were blocked (403) and commercial/academic datasets were not accessed.

Local search terms

  • middle mile — Backbone/transport fiber between the internet backbone and local networks; name of NTIA and California route datasets.
  • Awarded Route / Existing Route — Layer names in NTIA's Middle Mile public map services.
  • FiberOpticCable — Layer name used in DOT ITS asset services (e.g. Colorado ITS_Assets).
  • ITS fiber — Fiber of state DOT intelligent transportation systems along highways.
  • route miles — Unit used by CDT and NTIA tables for network length.
  • Ready-to-Connect — California MMBI status for tested, operational segments.
  • IRU — Indefeasible right of use; leased segments not distinguished on carrier maps.
  • CORA — Colorado Open Records Act request, CDOT's route for more fiber data.

Main caveats

  • Broadband availability (FCC National Broadband Map, Vermont 'Fiber Service Corridors') is not cable routing: Vermont's layer is E911 road segments where FTTH may be available.
  • NTIA layer status is 'Awarded' for all 35 projects; built miles (8,557 of 16,075) are project totals, not mapped segments.
  • California layer is a generalized design dissolved by county and status; Pre-Construction segments are not built.
  • State/city layers cover government-owned networks and vary in fields; several services block automated clients or need tokens.
  • Carrier maps (e.g. Lumen) are generalized and do not distinguish owned, leased and IRU segments.
