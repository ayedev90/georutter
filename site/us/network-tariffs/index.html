Confidence

There is no single national table. Transmission access rates are FERC-filed tariffs (eTariff, XML export, versions since 2010) and RTOs post computed rates: PJM zonal NITS in $/MW-year (XLSX), CAISO high/low-voltage access charges in $/MWh (PDF). Distribution charges are in each utility's state-approved tariff, e.g. Oncor (TX) by secondary/primary/transmission service and PG&E (CA) with distribution and transmission components by voltage and TOU period. OpenEI URDB compiles rates nationally but is mostly bundled retail rates.

Confidence reason: Sources tested with downloads, but distribution tariffs and RTO postings were sampled (3 utilities, 2 RTOs); PUCT summary blocked to automated clients.

Local search terms

  • Open Access Transmission Tariff (OATT) — FERC-filed tariff with transmission service terms and rates; searchable in eTariff.
  • Tariff for Retail Delivery Service — Texas TDU tariff containing distribution delivery charges by service voltage.
  • Unbundling of total rates — PG&E tariff section splitting each charge into generation, distribution and transmission components.
  • Network Integration Transmission Service (NITS) rate — PJM zonal transmission rate in $/MW-year posted by PJM settlements.
  • Transmission Access Charge (TAC) — CAISO high-voltage and low-voltage transmission charge in $/MWh.
  • TCRF / DCRF — Texas transmission and distribution cost recovery factor riders adjusting delivery charges.
  • TDU / TDSP — Transmission and distribution utility / service provider in Texas retail choice areas.
  • Secondary / Primary / Transmission voltage — Service voltage classes used in US retail tariffs; definitions vary by utility.

Main caveats

  • Transmission (FERC, wholesale) and distribution (state commissions, retail) are regulated separately; no source covers both nationally.
  • In vertically integrated states retail tariffs are bundled; a network-only charge may not exist as a published value.
  • Units differ: PJM $/MW-year, CAISO $/MWh, utility tariffs $/kW, $/kWh and $/customer; do not compare without conversion rules.
  • URDB is a convenience compilation; its voltage tags and start dates can differ from the official tariff.
